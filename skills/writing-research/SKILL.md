# Research Writing

Write research content a colleague can follow, from a paragraph to a shareable note. Keep supported findings, interpretations and unfinished work distinguishable. A complete paper template or completed proof is not required for a presentable research note.

## Read for the current work

- For composition or revision, read [composition and revision](references/composition-and-revision.md): argument, flexible note structure, author conventions and conservative editing.
- For mathematical or algorithmic exposition, also read [shared technical presentation](../doc-math/references/research-presentation.md). Formal development belongs to `doc-math`; clearer wording does not repair a proof.
- For source-file edits or a rendered deliverable, read [source and delivery](references/source-and-delivery.md): existing edits, LaTeX conventions and local verification.

Load only relevant references. Use this skill directly for ordinary academic passages, notes and memos. An active doc-* skill owns the document's special structure and overrides conflicting general conventions; do not force paper standards on ordinary notes.

## Workflow

1. Establish the reader, purpose, available evidence, language and revision scope. Reuse accepted voice, notation, macros, bibliography and useful structure; ask only material questions.
2. Organize the smallest coherent argument, draft connected prose and keep missing evidence explicit. Preserve proved/cited/empirical/inferred/conjectural/open status.
3. Read the requested scope once for meaning and flow; repair the smallest coherent passage. Return the requested prose without an unsolicited planning report or multi-pass style audit.
4. For publication papers, use `doc-paper` from the outline/first draft onward; it directly inherits this baseline. Use `review-manuscript` for dedicated post-draft paper audits or polish, not routine note feedback. Use `doc-literature`, `doc-math`, `doc-experiment` or `doc-meeting` for their specific document tasks when available.

Keep existing topic documents and project conventions; do not create parallel drafts, summaries or reports merely to organize an edit. Writing or checking files does not authorize external synchronization, publication or submission.
