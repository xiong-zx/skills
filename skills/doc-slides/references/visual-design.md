# Visual design for research slides

- Follow a supplied template's canvas, branding, and useful visual hierarchy. A deck provided to diagnose formulas is not permission to redesign its theme. Otherwise start with a restrained, legible 16:9 design suitable for projection.
- When visual direction is unsettled, preview a small number of representative pages before producing the whole deck. Include an equation-heavy page and a figure page; a polished cover alone does not test the design.
- Set consistent margins, alignment, title positions, body size, mathematical scale, and a small semantic palette. Check final-size screenshots; font size alone does not establish readability.
- Define a small typography preset before editing and use it consistently: for a compact research deck, a starting preset is 30 pt slide titles, 20 pt section headings/display equations, 18 pt body text/lists, and 16 pt chart labels/captions. Adapt the preset to the supplied template and canvas once, not separately for each textbox. Same-role text on one slide must not drift in size; use weight or color for emphasis. Rewrite, wrap or reposition before making a one-off font reduction. Native fractions and scripts retain their normal mathematical size hierarchy.
- Establish shared left edges, text baselines and a small spacing rhythm for comparable elements. Keep visible gaps between peer list items equal, with consistent hanging indents, paragraph spacing, text-box insets and vertical anchors. Equal box coordinates alone do not ensure equal visual spacing: top-aligned and vertically centered text can differ. For multi-line items, measure the gap after the final visible line rather than blindly equalizing item starts. Reuse heading-to-body and block-to-block gaps across comparable slides; retain intentionally larger section gaps. Verify these relationships in the exported slide and correct obvious inconsistencies before delivery.
- Make alignment instrumented by default when editing layout. Read actual shape geometry, parent-group transforms, text insets, anchors and paragraph spacing before choosing common edges, centers, baselines and item gaps. Apply the app's Align/Distribute tools or deterministic coordinate changes with shared anchors; avoid a sequence of unmeasured visual nudges. Measure the affected objects again and inspect a fresh native render. For assessment-only work, measure and report without moving objects.
- Align equations at their meaningful relation signs when appropriate, rather than forcing their text frames to the same left edge. Use rendered text/glyph bounds when available to distinguish visible alignment from box alignment. Preserve intentional offsets and figure overlaps. A clean generic geometry-validator report does not establish alignment or readability; document the unavailable measurement instead of claiming it was checked.
- Prefer native bulleted lists for distinct reasons/comparisons and numbered lists for algorithm steps or a sequence. Keep items concise and parallel, with consistent indentation and spacing. Do not turn a single sentence, a diagram label or a tightly connected argument into a list merely to increase the number of bullets.
- Avoid long paragraphs on visible slides. Use short phrases, lists and compact symbolic chains when they communicate the mechanism more directly. Implication (=>), equivalence (<=>), approximation and similar symbols are welcome; do not require full sentences. Preserve the distinction between a justified equivalence, an approximation, a tuning choice and a causal claim. Put supporting explanations in narration/notes instead of expanding every chain into prose.
- Define colors by semantic role before styling: use the same color for the same algorithm or object in text, formulas, diagrams, legends, and plots throughout a deck. Keep algorithm identities distinct from other roles such as noise components; do not accidentally give one color conflicting meanings. Theme alternatives may change the palette, but must preserve its role mapping.
- Use a consistent capitalization convention for titles, labels, and algorithm names; preserve official names and quoted publication titles. Distinguish primary plotted methods with equally prominent styles. Reserve dashed or muted lines for reference quantities when that is the intended meaning, rather than making a main comparator look secondary.
- Organize one dominant explanatory task per slide. Add diagrams when relationships are clearer visually, not to satisfy a universal image quota. Avoid unnecessary card grids, badges, decorative icons, gradients, or motion.
- Keep ordinary prose as native text. Preserve original quantitative figure assets or regenerate deterministically from recorded data; do not generatively redraw results. Simplify labels for slides without removing units, comparisons, or uncertainty that changes interpretation.
- Keep formula and body font families compatible, but allow the mathematical font needed for proper typesetting. Use the equation reference for glyph sizing, alignment, and source retention rather than styling math as ordinary body text.
- Use short local annotations to direct attention. Keep notation and color meanings stable across progressive builds. Animations should expose a mechanism or proof step and should have a usable static/PDF fallback when needed.
- Do not put completeness-only qualifications on the visible slide by default. Keep fixed-scale conventions, technical scope reminders, and similar supporting caveats in speaker notes or the planned narration when they do not change the audience's takeaway. Keep a qualification visible when omitting it would materially mislead the audience.
- Cut or split overloaded content before shrinking text. Check projector contrast, small indices, figure legends, and color-only distinctions. White space is a consequence of a clear hierarchy, not a mandatory percentage.
- For HTML or PDF, preserve the intended slide geometry and verify exports. Do not assume browser responsiveness, mathematical rendering, or animations survive a PPTX conversion.

Selective inspiration: [frontend-slides](https://github.com/zarazhangrui/frontend-slides) for visual previews, speaking-versus-reading density, and viewport discipline; [scientific-slides](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/tree/main/skills/scientific-slides) for research figures and visual validation. Generic font bans, decorative requirements, and fixed content quotas are not adopted. Existing `plot-paper` governs standalone publication figures; slides may need a different viewing scale.
