# Purpose, outline, and flow

- Start from the actual purpose, occasion, topic, and audience. Progress updates, technical discussions, literature briefings, teaching, conference talks, and defenses can need different organizations. Do not assume every deck must tell a research story, establish novelty, or culminate in one contribution.
- Determine whether the deck supports live speech, asynchronous reading, or later reference. A speaking deck can rely on explanation; a reading deck needs more self-contained context. Do not force the same density on both.
- Treat a confirmed outline as the working contract. Fill its content and improve local transitions; surface material changes to argument, emphasis, or claimed result before making them.
- For an unsettled outline, organize the supplied material around what the audience needs to understand, discuss, learn, or decide. A problem-to-mechanism-to-result arc is one option for a suitable research talk, not an intake gate or universal template. Allow an initial draft from rough material before refining the whole-deck flow.
- Prefer a concrete example or local model before dense notation when it improves understanding. Introduce symbols where first used and reuse names and color semantics consistently.
- Give each slide an identifiable purpose: question, setup, mechanism, result, comparison, or synthesis. An evidence-backed finding can have a takeaway title; a definition or setup slide may use a direct subject title. Do not manufacture a claim for every title.
- During review, check whether a reader can follow the sequence: what context is available, why does the next section belong here, and are symbols or prerequisites introduced before use? Flag abrupt jumps, missing context, or unnecessary repetition. Legitimately separate topics can be grouped and signposted without inventing a causal storyline between them.
- Allocate time by explanatory difficulty, including transitions and any in-slot questions. A complex theorem or figure may need much longer than a transition slide. Rehearsal, not a fixed slides-per-minute ratio, determines fit.
- Distinguish essential slides from backup proof details, full assumptions, extended results, and likely Q&A where relevant. When timing matters, identify a shorter route that preserves the deck's actual purpose.
- Preserve evidence boundaries: illustration versus data, intuition versus proof, known result versus contribution, and supported conclusion versus conjecture. Do not invent numbers, novelty, comparisons, or citations to make the presentation smoother.

Selective inspiration from [K-Dense scientific-slides](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/tree/main/skills/scientific-slides) and [EvoSkills academic-slides](https://github.com/EvoScientist/EvoSkills/tree/main/skills/academic-slides). Their talk-organization ideas are optional tools. No mandatory research arc, fixed slide counts, citation quotas, new literature search, or automatic full-slide image generation is adopted.
