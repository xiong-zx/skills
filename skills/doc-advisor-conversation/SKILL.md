# Advisor Conversation Document

## Preserve the meeting boundary

- Inspect the live folder and applicable `AGENTS.md` before editing. Current filenames are authoritative.
- Identify the intended advisor and topic before choosing a target file.
- Keep different advisors or substantively different meetings in separate idea streams and drafts. Never merge them merely because both concern research or career decisions.

## Capture mode

When the user is still sending thoughts:

- Append them chronologically to the target meeting's raw-ideas file.
- Preserve the user's meaning, contradictions, uncertainty, incomplete phrases, and approximate dates.
- Make only safe transcription corrections. Mark ambiguous wording instead of inventing an interpretation.
- Do not prematurely summarize, diagnose, decide, or revise the final meeting document.

## Draft mode

Enter draft mode only when the user explicitly asks to prepare or update the meeting document.

Produce a **talking-prompt card, not a script**:

- Aim for one page or one quick screen.
- Use short bullets and keywords that can be understood at a glance.
- Keep headings few and shallow; avoid paragraphs of polished exposition.
- Make each section independently skippable so the user can reorder topics in response to the advisor.
- Preserve natural first-person language and leave room for spontaneous explanation.

A useful default shape is:

1. one- or two-line opening;
2. three to five current-situation prompts;
3. the user's present interpretation or tensions;
4. four to six focused questions for the advisor;
5. two to four desired meeting outcomes.

Adapt or omit sections when a shorter shape works better.

## Editorial boundaries

- When `$writing-research` is explicitly combined with this skill, use it only as a general clarity baseline; this skill's first-person, conversational, sensitive-topic, and talking-prompt rules override any conflicting academic-writing rule.
- Distinguish personal experience, interpretation, factual claim, and future prediction.
- Preserve exact scientific claims, numbers, citations, and evidence status when research content appears in the conversation.
- Keep observation, interpretation, uncertainty, and requested advice distinct even when compressing them into short prompts.
- Do not turn setbacks, peer pressure, low motivation, or an AI forecast into a conclusion about ability or career fit.
- Rephrase sensitive comments respectfully for the advisor-facing draft without erasing the underlying issue.
- Do not make the user's decision. Frame unresolved choices as answerable questions.
- Keep raw notes intact unless the user explicitly asks to revise or remove them.

## Verification

Before finishing:

- Confirm only the intended meeting stream changed.
- Check that the draft is materially shorter than the raw notes and can be scanned during live conversation.
- Remove duplicated context, nested analysis, and sentences the user would need to read verbatim.
- Verify filenames and any internal references after renames.
