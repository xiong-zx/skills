# Self-contained research questions

Make each card usable in a new chat that sees no earlier messages or cards. Repeat the small amount of common setup needed to interpret it; do not say "same model as above."

Include:

- one bounded scientific question and its relationship to the larger program;
- objective, domain, dimensions, random variables, conditioning, and whether subspaces are fixed;
- exact data/oracle access and the observation budget;
- parameters and geometry known to the algorithm versus only to the analyst;
- permitted update and controls, including the timing of adaptive choices;
- exact output metric and how outputs are chosen;
- a fair baseline with comparable information and oracle budget;
- requested outputs: identity, upper bound, lower bound, counterexample, necessary assumptions, or a justified failure diagnosis;
- what is assumed, already derived, proposed, or not yet established.

Change one substantive difficulty at a time when possible. Give simple controls a chance to solve the question before requiring a new method. A known averaging identity or symmetry argument can expose a trivial stage; label it as a baseline rather than presenting it as a contribution.

Separate lack of information from lack of algorithmic control. Compare an unknown-geometry scalar rule with an informed scalar rule to study information, and informed scalar versus informed blockwise updates to study actuation. Do not give a comparator the true solution or extra samples by accident.

Do not call uncorrelated coordinates independent, or a rotated separable model a genuinely interacting objective. For heavy tails, verify that the metric exists. For local loss geometry, name the region and account for exits instead of assuming an unbounded-noise trajectory stays there.

Invite a negative result when the stated model cannot support the intended conclusion. Do not ask for a predetermined improved rate or claim the card is a globally open problem without evidence.
