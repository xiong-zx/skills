# Paper contract, contributions and readiness

Identify the publication purpose or intended venue, audience, paper type, language, length and current task: drafting, revising or finalizing. Drafting may proceed before venue selection; final venue readiness requires a known target. For venue-specific work, verify the current official template and requirements, including anonymity, references, figures, supplements and page limits.

State the research question, central answer, novelty boundary, demonstrated scope and main contributions. Reuse the authorized corpus, mathematical results and experiment records rather than silently starting a new literature review or changing the scientific contract.

## Frame claims and contributions

Map every headline claim to an inspectable result/support later in the paper: a theorem with a complete proof, an exact external result, a validated experiment/artifact, or a clearly identified conjecture/interpretation. Keep the following roles distinct:

- **Setting:** problem, feedback, information and applicable regime.
- **Contribution:** new method, result or understanding the paper establishes; a new algorithm is not compulsory.
- **Advantage:** when it is useful or how it compares under stated conditions.

A fact may have multiple roles. Suitability for a setting or an anticipated benefit is not a proved contribution. Comparative advantages require theory/experiments that respect differences in assumptions, feedback, accuracy criteria and costs.

Lead with the useful regime and supported strengths. Put material assumptions, oracle requirements, cost conventions and limitations beside the claims they qualify. Avoid generic self-deprecation while preserving negative findings and necessary qualifications; stronger on one dimension does not imply universal superiority.

Use the [mathematical exposition guidance](mathematical-exposition.md#3-arrange-the-reading-path) for presenting assumptions and setup.

## Evidence and readiness

- Each established central result needs a complete argument or an exact verified external dependency. An overview or labeled sketch helps exposition but does not discharge the proof obligation.
- Give algorithms inspectable implementations or [pseudocode](mathematical-exposition.md#5-present-pseudocode-readers-can-follow) with any accuracy or stopping certificates the claims require. Mark unresolved solver obligations.
- Empirical claims need data, configurations, fair comparisons, metrics, uncertainty and reproducibility evidence. Make figure, table, caption and prose agree; include controls/ablations when the inference depends on them.
- Verify load-bearing literature/novelty claims with appropriate result locators; a failed search does not establish novelty. Conjectures and open problems may remain, but cannot support claims presented as established contributions.

Before claiming submission readiness, confirm:

1. headline contributions have adequate evidence and accurate scope;
2. definitions, assumptions, methods, results and proof/experiment dependencies agree across sections;
3. no unlabeled gap, placeholder, unresolved citation or incomplete artifact supports a central claim;
4. the main text exposes conditions needed to interpret results even when detail is deferred;
5. final files meet the identified venue's formatting, length, anonymity, citation and supplement rules.

During drafting, keep missing evidence explicit; during finalization, resolve its support or remove the unsupported contribution. Use writing-research's source/render checks and review-manuscript for authorized post-draft audits/polish. A blocked readiness check reports exact remaining items, not a polished but unsupported ready verdict.
