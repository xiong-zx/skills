# Reader and technical presentation review

Read this for the first two passes of a paper-standard review. For detailed mathematical statement/proof/notation findings, use [proof-readability.md](proof-readability.md) rather than duplicating a general-language pass. Use the intended venue's reader as the lens, not the author's accumulated project knowledge. A manuscript can be technically correct yet force the reader to reconstruct its argument.

## First pass: entry, argument, and results

- Skim the title, Abstract, headings, figures, tables, and captions before reading linearly. Record the apparent problem, information available to the method, central answer, decisive evidence, and limits. Then read the paper and note where that initial map must be revised.
- Track the reader's question and each section's role. At meaningful task changes, look for avoidable loss of context, missing prerequisites, or an unexplained new target. Paragraph-level handoffs are guidance: do not demand a transition at every boundary or a causal link between parallel tasks. For mathematical ordering and progression use the specialized reference; a fluent connective does not repair a missing dependency.
- Distinguish the setting from an established contribution and from a supported advantage. A useful regime or hoped-for benefit is not itself a proved result. The title, Abstract, Introduction, results, and closing sections should tell the same story at the same claim strength.
- Explain the method's central operation and why it addresses the stated obstacle before judging prose details. Check whether the proof idea or experiments actually connect the intermediate results to the final claim, without mistaking an overview for a full proof.
- Skim a theoretical-analysis section through its headings and formal statements. Apply the specialized exposition reference's main-text selection and result-role tests: can readers identify the central answer and supporting tasks? Do not demand a fixed result map or infer novelty/correctness from an environment name.
- After each central result, assess whether the local interpretation explains its contribution, important terms and supported advantage over the closest literature, without requiring the reader to remember the Introduction. A comparison must name its dimension and reason, preserve differences in assumptions, feedback, metrics and costs, and avoid an unsupported claim of universal superiority. Report excessive auxiliary exposition when it displaces this interpretation; retain essential conditions and proof obligations.
- State a formal target with its quantity, assumptions, regime, randomness, and output. Distinguish randomness in the observations, algorithm, and output selection. Do not convert a high-probability result into an expectation claim without controlling the failure event. Compare prior work only under matching feedback, assumptions, accuracy criteria, and costs. When a definition says it follows a source, check that the main text states the material differences there. When models or protocols are compared, check that each simulation states which model runs which algorithm and at what cost; an inclusion diagram or "by inclusion" is not the reason.
- Check that a Related Work subsection or other survey transition gives the reader a comparison question before its citation-led paragraphs. In preliminaries, distinguish core concepts needed for the main argument from routine optimality or supporting proof details that can be deferred without hiding conditions. For this author, flag crowded inline definitions and target criteria that need displayed math, and verify that every multiline display has a number even when unreferenced. Do not treat fewer equation numbers as an independent writing objective. Check a complete inventory of display math, including parallel formulas written on one source line and displays that wrap in the PDF; a search for explicit line-break commands cannot establish complete numbering coverage.
- Look at rendered pages when visual communication matters. A figure-first reader should recognize the comparison, axes and units, uncertainty, and principal observation from the figure and caption. Check whether size, panel density, notation, and legends direct attention to the evidence.
- Prioritize the few obstacles that change the central reading path. Do not demand background for every standard concept, arbitrary paragraph lengths, a figure in every section, or a lengthy section-by-section paraphrase.

## Second pass: technical exposition and organization

- Follow formulation, assumptions, algorithms, theorems, and equations as one system. Use the [mathematical exposition review](proof-readability.md) to trace definitions, notation, information dependencies, output and cost.
- For quantitative claims, check the finite-horizon inequality, declared constant dependencies and fixed versus varying parameters. Require a definition before the first asymptotic notation, and explicit logarithmic factors in this author's formal rate statements instead of soft-O. Distinguish a sufficient horizon from an order upper bound, and a separate guarantee for each chosen horizon from a simultaneous event across horizons. A generic constant with declared dependencies is not an explicit numerical calibration; flag any wording that suggests otherwise.
- Distinguish quantities computed by the algorithm from quantities introduced only to analyze it. Check whether a method section explains the operational choice before introducing proof bookkeeping, and whether a proof appendix states the target implication before introducing its auxiliary estimates. A technical appendix may legitimately discuss proof difficulties; the problem motivation should not silently substitute them for method-design difficulties.
- Check the author's chosen names and grammatical roles across the supplied material: distinguish the decision that is deployed, the scenarios that are sampled and the objective that is minimized. Introduce unfamiliar shorthand in concrete terms before using it to carry an inference; report term drift with the affected locations rather than imposing a universal word ban. For this author, check that each technical term is explained and set in bold at its first substantive use, and that lemma and proposition titles are noun phrases.
- When numerical consolidation is requested, give a keep/merge/move/cut plan organized by evaluation question. Distinguish decisive main-text evidence from full protocols, breakdowns and diagnostics suitable for the appendix; retain key exceptions, statistical units, budget conventions and theory/implementation differences. Check that boldface and other rankings name their comparison group rather than hide a better result in another group.
- Flag a notation or formulation change that would alter assumptions, guarantees, or proof obligations. Do not present it as a cosmetic edit. For disputed correctness, request a specialist proof audit and state what remains unchecked.

For each finding, give a location, the concrete obstacle, its consequence for reading or interpretation, and the smallest useful revision. Preserve a successful presentation choice when it helps the author avoid undoing it.
