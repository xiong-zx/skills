# Academic language diagnosis examples

These are constructed examples, not claims or quotations from a manuscript. Use them to distinguish a writing obstacle from a necessary scientific expression. Proposed edits depend on the actual surrounding evidence.

## Direct operation

Source: "We conduct an evaluation of the method under label noise."

Safe polish: "We evaluate the method under label noise."

The verb becomes direct without adding a setting, finding, or claim of robustness.

## Necessary contrast

Source: "Our guarantee concerns the true objective rather than the fitted surrogate."

Keep when this distinction is established by the theorem. Both objects and the exclusion carry technical information; deleting the negative half could change the guarantee.

## Empty opposition with a substantive claim

Source: "This is not merely an implementation improvement, but a fundamentally new learning paradigm."

Flag the undefined comparison and novelty claim. Identify the actual contribution and its support before suggesting a factual replacement. A reviewer must not quietly erase the novelty claim while pretending only to polish language.

## Supported additive claims

Source: "The method not only lowers memory usage but also reduces latency."

Possible polish: "The method lowers memory usage and reduces latency."

Preserve both comparisons and their conditions. If either is unsupported, flag its support separately; removing the rhetorical form does not resolve the evidence question.

## Deduction or causal explanation

Source: "The estimator has lower variance; therefore, the algorithm converges faster."

Check whether an applicable convergence argument or relevant experiment supports the link. Lower variance alone is not a complete justification. Preserve or flag the inference until it is checked; adding or deleting therefore is not automatically a harmless edit.

## Commentary attached to evidence

Source: "Removing the term raises error, highlighting the elegance of the design."

Possible polish: "Removing the term raises error."

The elegance comment supplies no mechanism or comparison. Preserve any actual error estimate, uncertainty, conditions, and citation in the source.

## A fluent change that alters aggregation

Source: "The method has higher mean accuracy across tasks."

Unsafe rewrite: "The method consistently outperforms the baselines."

An aggregate mean does not imply improvement on every task. The rewrite also introduces a potentially different comparator and broadens the performance metric.

## Functional repetition

Several theorem cases begin with "If", or several experiment paragraphs begin with "We evaluate".

Repetition alone is insufficient. Keep the parallel form when it helps readers compare genuine cases. Flag a cluster only if the repeated introductions or closers add redundant rhetorical work or hide each paragraph's distinct question.
