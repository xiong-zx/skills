# Post-draft language audit and polish

Use for a paper's standalone language audit, requested polish, or the language pass of a full review. Read the supplied scope in context. Use the author's accepted prose or relevant disciplinary convention when available; an author sample is helpful but is not a prerequisite. For difficult cases, read [language-examples.md](language-examples.md).

## Establish the meaning before judging style

Identify what the passage asserts, its logical role, evidence status, and scope. A fluent draft records what the author says; it does not establish that the claim or citation has been verified. Preserve formulas, numbers, units, negation, quantifiers, modality, causal strength, comparator, aggregation unit, assumptions, citations, and formal names.

## Local diagnosis

- **Find the proposition and control processing load.** Can the reader identify the subject, operation, object and conclusion? Flag buried verbs, chains of abstract nouns, stacked qualifiers, ambiguous modifier attachment, invented labels and explanations that merely repeat a formula. Split a sentence when several distinct claims overload it; join fragments when they express one connected step. Neither short sentences nor active voice are universal goals. An algorithm, estimator or bound can be a legitimate technical subject. Preserve useful mathematical compression.
- **Check idiomatic academic English sentence by sentence.** Inspect articles, number, tense, agreement, prepositions, disciplinary collocations, parallel structure and comparison syntax. Diagnose grammatically possible but unnatural phrasing too. Name the precise problem and supply an idiomatic replacement; do not intensify the claim, casualize the register or change technical terms to make the prose sound human.
- **Recover context and purpose.** Locate undefined objects, ambiguous "this", definitions needed many pages earlier, and paragraphs doing several jobs without a visible progression. State what the reader must look up or infer. A short role reminder and an exact existing label often work better than another definition, an indirect “bound after” reference or a repeated long formula.
- **Check the logical relation.** Identify whether adjacent statements express addition, dependency, deduction, contrast, concession, chronology, or an empirical explanation. Check whether therefore, furthermore, because, or a trailing -ing clause expresses that actual relation. Name both objects when “its increase” or a similar pronoun could reverse a comparison or operation. Chronology does not establish causation; mathematical deduction is assessed through the argument. A new connective must not invent a relation.
- **Ground a contrast.** Identify both propositions and the comparison dimension. Keep a distinction supported by definitions, prior work, hypotheses, ablations, or scope. Remove empty opposition only when no material proposition is lost. Preserve both claims in a genuine additive contrast. A concrete but unverified contrast is uncertain and needs support, not automatic deletion.
- **Test generic importance.** Ask whether a sentence could move unchanged to an unrelated paper. Locate the specific mechanism, result, condition, or consequence already available in the text. Flag ceremonial openings, staged insights, inflated significance, vague attribution, and trailing commentary that adds no information. Do not invent a more concrete fact or quietly erase a novelty or impact claim to make it sound safer.
- **Track object identity.** Keep one accepted term per scientific object, with declared abbreviations and grammatical variants. Flag confirmed synonym drift across text and captions. Check distinctions such as signed measure versus probability distribution, sampling noise versus observed response, and shared feedback model versus a shared realized data history. Preserve intentional distinctions and coined method names; similar wording alone does not establish that two objects are identical.
- **Separate identities, interpretations and guarantees.** In a technical passage, locate which statements are exact formulas for a fitted object, which are conditional interpretations of the true object, and which are supported accuracy claims. Flag unqualified estimation or guarantee language when the supplied evidence controls a different quantity, such as values rather than derivatives. Put necessary conditions beside the interpretation; a correction requiring a new assumption or proof is a technical issue, not merely a prose improvement.

## Paragraph and document diagnosis

Within the material actually supplied, inspect repeated openings, identical problem-method-benefit-summary shapes, automatic paragraph closers, and equally weighted treatment of unequal evidence. A repeated shape is a defect when its rhetorical function is redundant or obscures the argument, not merely because it occurs several times. Methods steps, theorem cases, ablations, and parallel conditions may correctly repeat their form.

For complete sections or manuscripts, identify each section's reader question and what the reader should learn. Notice component inventories repeated in the Abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion; experiments introduced without an evaluation question; controls distant from the alternative they address; and exposition following project history instead of conceptual dependencies. Recommend the smallest structural correction supported by the existing text. A language-only task does not authorize automatic manuscript reconstruction. An excerpt supports only an excerpt-level finding.

For a reorganization or condensation, distinguish moved, deleted and merged passages from rewritten prose. Flag incidental sentence-by-sentence paraphrases when the source wording works, and check both sides of each move for missing definitions, antecedents, conditions and references. Audit the resulting reading path as well as the highlighted changes. Compare condensed sentences with their sources for lost qualifiers and premises, such as "can", "unless", "at most" or "independent of the history"; dropping one strengthens the claim. When several editors changed the text, attribute each change to the version that introduced it before judging it.

For appendices, distinguish useful proof signposting and reproducibility detail from duplicated proof inventories, self-announcing openings and formulaic closing sentences. Recommend located cuts or rewrites that improve the reader's progression, while preserving hypotheses, quantifiers, evidence status and the steps needed to follow the argument. Do not make every section adopt the same polished paragraph pattern.

## Protect useful academic expression

Preserve technical terms, legitimate nominalization, purposeful signposting, precise exclusions, theorem-level certainty, and uncertainty justified by evidence. Passive voice can correctly foreground a procedure or result. Real contrasts, contribution lists, and parallel mathematical cases can be necessary. Match the author's register and punctuation when they work.

For this author, flag frequent clause-joining semicolons, fragmented sequences of short sentences, comma-heavy clause chains and nonessential appositives that interrupt the main proposition. Prefer complete, connected sentences without forcing one claim per sentence. Semicolons remain appropriate when they clarify closely related clauses or complex lists, and mathematical delimiters must be retained. After splitting or joining sentences, reread the paragraph for its subjects, antecedents, qualifications and logical connections rather than mechanically changing punctuation.

Do not use a banned-word list, punctuation quota, hedge count, sentence-length threshold, or target for artificial variation. Do not introduce personal anecdotes, deliberate errors, casual speech, or decoration to make prose appear human. Describe observable writing obstacles without estimating AI authorship.

## Review or revise within the request

- For audit or feedback, return located findings with the exact source wording, the proposition or relation at issue, the reader's likely misunderstanding and a ready-to-use replacement when requested. Distinguish language, organization and technically uncertain findings. If repairing continuity requires a paragraph rewrite, provide the complete affected paragraph or passage rather than scattered word substitutions, and explain its connection to the surrounding text. Keep unresolved scientific changes outside the proposed clean wording. The proposed replacement remains a suggestion until editing is authorized. Classify candidates as change, keep, or uncertain; report material problems and useful exceptions without cataloguing every good sentence. Separate wording obstacles from evidence gaps.
- A polish, rewrite, or fix request authorizes edits to the requested prose. Fix grammar, idiom, and unsupported rhetorical scaffolding with the smallest coherent change; a paragraph may need rewriting when phrase substitutions leave the obstacle intact. Leave effective prose alone.
- For Chinese-to-English revision, recover the intended proposition and relations before composing idiomatic English. Preserve technical vocabulary, scope, and hedges; do not infer causation from an implicit transition.
- If a correction would change a scientific claim or require a missing fact, source, mechanism, or result, flag that item and continue other supported edits. Do not disguise a substantive correction as language polish. Apply claim changes only within explicit authorization and inspected evidence.

## Recheck and deliver

Compare each substantive proposed or applied edit with its source. Verify that all material propositions remain, including both sides of comparisons; negation, quantifiers, modality, comparator, aggregation, evidence strength, and citation scope must survive. Recheck adjacent text and repeated patterns so that a repair neither breaks the argument nor creates another mechanical template. Inspect numerical differences and units when prose depends on them.

For polish, return clean revised prose or the requested file change, with brief notes on meaning-risk edits and unresolved support. For an audit, return findings without changing the source. Do not claim a whole-manuscript pass for an excerpt, or manufacture an aesthetic score. Compile and inspect affected pages when editing LaTeX files.
