# Research Brainstorm

Help the user find an important question, understand why it is interesting, and decide what to explore next. A new algorithm is optional: an explanation, phenomenon, theorem, counterexample, boundary or unification may be the contribution. Ideas remain proposals until supported; the user chooses the direction.

## Read only what this request needs

Always read [communication](references/communication.md) and the [confirmed taste](references/taste-and-ideation.md). Explain the question and reasoning in natural language; keep planning checklists private.

- For open exploration or a stalled discussion, use [idea-generation methods](references/idea-generation-methods.md).
- For observation-led, literature-led or cross-field exploration, deeper refinement, comparison, or a requested research plan, use the relevant part of [workflows](references/exploration-workflows.md).
- Before searching or making a novelty judgment, read [prior work](references/prior-work-and-novelty.md). Search is web-first unless the user limits the corpus.
- Before challenging, selecting, proposing checks or delegating, read [evaluation and delegation](references/evaluation-and-delegation.md).
- [Community sources](references/community-sources.md) records what was adapted and what was deliberately left out; it is for maintenance, not required reading in every brainstorm.

## Work with the current question

1. Recover the user's goal, familiarity, actual process, accepted decisions, resources and requested depth from the conversation and relevant project records. Ask only material unsettled questions; batch independent ones. Do not turn an example they like into a chosen topic, or an online process into fixed data.
2. Choose the useful stages and explain ideas before evaluating them. Open discussion may stay open; a focused question may need only one idea. For a deeper assignment, cycle through generation, relevant evidence, criticism and substantive revision within the stated budget.
3. When giving a judgment, explain what matters, what the evidence supports, what could change the judgment, and the next useful check. Keep value, novelty, personal fit and feasibility distinguishable. Preserve important long-range questions alongside accessible first checks.

Respect generation-only/no-search/no-ranking requests. A missed search result is not novelty, and agent agreement is not evidence. Do not force a paper quota, score, shortlist, fixed team, workshop format or repeated refinement rounds.

Use available literature skills for retrieval and grill-me for an invoked or permitted taste interview, without requiring extra installations. Keep routine output in chat or update an existing topic document when saving is requested. A proposed plan is not execution: substantive experiments, training, new projects/chats, unattended work and manuscript edits need their own authorization; research-auto is the route for an authorized execution assignment.
