# Prior work and evidence-bounded novelty

## Retrieve only what the decision needs

Start web-first with primary papers, preprints, proceedings, journals and author pages, not Zotero by default. Respect an explicit existing-corpus/no-new-search boundary; then state that external coverage remains unchecked. Use `literature-fast` for a bounded first pass and `literature-review` when substantial independent topics, citation expansion or deeper verification are needed. Missing sibling skills do not authorize installation; perform an equivalent bounded search with available tools.

Search multiple expressions of the problem, broader area, mechanism and equivalent terminology. Check plausible older formulations and adjacent disciplines, not just recent papers sharing the current label. Follow the strongest seeds backward/forward when useful. Search contrary explanations and negative evidence as well as supportive results.

Deduplicate preprint/publication lineages. Model recall is a lead, not a verified citation. Resolve the actual paper/version and inspect its text before relying on a substantive scientific claim. Search snippets and abstracts may screen candidates but cannot establish detailed theorem assumptions, mechanism claims or absence of prior art.

Use the existing retrieval skill's evidence labels, or their explicit equivalents:

- `DISCOVERED`: metadata/search result only.
- `ABSTRACT_ONLY`: title/abstract inspected.
- `PARTIAL_TEXT`: identified sections/passages inspected.
- `FULL_TEXT_VERIFIED`: text needed for every reported substantive claim was checked.

Record stable links, versions and exact sections/theorems/figures for load-bearing nearest-work claims. An inaccessible paper, terminology gap or missing citation chain is an open uncertainty, not support for novelty. Do not upload private manuscripts/data to a service without authorization.

## State the actual delta

For promising directions, compare with the strongest plausible near-equivalent work, not only weak baselines. Align, as relevant:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Problem/phenomenon | Is the target actually different, or just renamed? |
| Assumptions and scope | What is weaker, stronger or newly addressed? |
| Information and resources | Are oracle access, data, queries, compute and budgets comparable? |
| Mechanism or explanation | What new understanding is claimed; what alternative already explains it? |
| Result and evidence | What conclusion, prediction, bound, counterexample or measurement changes? |

For a mechanism-only project, the delta may be an explanation with new implications, a corrected idealization, a discriminating observation or a unified account, not an algorithm. Explain why that difference matters. A change of application alone is not automatically a valuable contribution; nor is a proof automatically equivalent just because the algorithm is unchanged.

Distinguish three evidence states without turning them into a novelty ranking:

- **Located overlap:** specific work may already establish the claimed contribution; explain the overlap and any remaining difference.
- **Possible substantive delta:** a difference is articulated, with its supporting evidence and unresolved checks.
- **Unresolved search:** no direct match found within the stated boundary, or key sources remain unchecked; this does not establish novelty.

State what could overturn the assessment and which next source/check is most useful. A confident-sounding novelty score, overlap-axis count or publication forecast is not a substitute for this comparison.

## Budget and stop

Use an agreed budget or a clearly stated bounded first pass. Deepen only the most decision-relevant candidates; do not require a fixed paper count or full systematic survey. Stop when the budget is reached or marginal searches mostly repeat known evidence. If the scope must expand materially, report the coverage gap and ask rather than silently spending indefinitely. Do not label bounded retrieval exhaustive, systematic, or global novelty verification.
