# Taste and open ideation

## Confirmed user defaults

These preferences were confirmed for this user's research brainstorming, not asserted as universal rules. A current explicit instruction can revise them; never infer permission for a costly run from discussion alone.

- Prioritize an important problem and a substantive new mechanism or understanding. An unstudied corner or a fashionable keyword is not enough.
- Mechanism analysis is a standalone contribution: explaining an existing method, exposing an invalid simplifying assumption, identifying a failure boundary or unifying conflicting explanations need not introduce a new algorithm. Likewise, do not require every theoretical question to start with an experiment.
- Theory and empirical research are both allowed; choose the approach the question needs. Neither a formal theorem nor a benchmark improvement is a universal entry requirement.
- Keep important questions that need substantial further work alongside questions whose key uncertainty has a small decisive check. Explain what work remains. A quick check does not imply a quick or low-impact paper, and difficulty alone is not a reason to discard an important question.
- Exclude directions requiring large-scale GPU training without the user's resources. Do not make successful RL training/performance the main bet or prerequisite for the contribution. This does not categorically exclude RL theory or analysis of existing evidence.
- Do not propose Muon as a current topic by default. The user's reported observation of dynamics outside a fixed-singular-frame assumption illustrates their taste; it is not independently verified evidence or a request to pursue Muon.
- Crowded fields and contradictory results raise differentiation and verification costs. Do not automatically present a contradiction as an opportunity: ask whether the key uncertainty can be resolved with accessible evidence and modest resources.

## Clarify only what is unsettled

Recover relevant existing notes and prior user decisions before interviewing. If necessary, ask compact batches of independent high-value questions; postpone questions that depend on unanswered ones. Facts are the agent's job to investigate. Use `grill-me` when the user has invoked or permitted it; otherwise ask focused clarification directly rather than bypassing its explicit-invocation boundary.

Concrete examples of attractive/unattractive work can expose taste better than generic adjectives. Reflect back the principle and let the user correct it. Label inferred preferences as tentative; do not repeat settled questions, prescribe the user's taste, or force an exhaustive interview before useful exploration. Recommendations should explain consequences, not make agreement the only easy answer.

For a new assignment, clarify only material unknowns such as whether the task is broad discovery or a cut within an existing direction, what resources are actually accessible, and whether a near-term decision or longer exploration is wanted. Do not invent a hardware/time budget.

## Diverge, then revisit

- Formulate several distinct questions before choosing methods. Differentiate underlying phenomena, assumptions, mechanisms or information conditions, not titles or cosmetic algorithm changes.
- Allow phenomenon-led, theory-led and practical-problem-led starting points. Separate what was observed from a proposed explanation; a pretty mechanism story is still a hypothesis.
- Initial independent generation may precede expanded searching when the field context is adequate. Do not suppress known prior art or guess facts merely to avoid anchoring. Limited background retrieval is appropriate when the question cannot be framed responsibly without it.
- Cluster by substantive relation, retaining alternatives whose assumptions or implications differ. Preserve unusual/high-risk candidates through the first pass instead of letting immediate feasibility criticism erase all ambition.
- After bounded prior-work checks, deliberately revisit the candidate set. A known problem can still support a valuable weaker-assumption result, sharper boundary, new explanation or simpler unification; existing work on the same problem is not an automatic rejection.
- Treat mechanism combinations as candidates only when the interaction itself has a reason to matter. Do not substitute a bag of techniques for a research question.
