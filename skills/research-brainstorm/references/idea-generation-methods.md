# Ways to find a question worth exploring

Choose a useful approach for the blockage. These are optional thinking moves, not a sequence, required number of ideas, weighted random sampler or guarantee of creativity. Keep the user's confirmed taste and actual resources.

## Begin with the question, not a toolbox

An attractive technique can be a lead, but explain what scientific question would remain interesting if that technique were unavailable. Conversely, an existing method can be the right object of research when its behavior remains unexplained. At this stage, a plausible mechanism family or a precise unresolved question is enough; do not require a finished algorithm, proof or experimental result.

| Starting point | Useful move | What the move should clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An observation does not fit the explanation | Generate competing explanations, including measurement or implementation artifacts | What each account predicts differently and what observation could distinguish them |
| An assumption looks unrealistic | Remove or vary that assumption while retaining the others | Where a conclusion actually depends on it, and why the new regime matters |
| A broad goal feels remote | Work backward from the desired capability or understanding | Which missing result blocks progress and whether it is really necessary |
| Several papers appear to disagree | Align definitions, assumptions, data, budgets and metrics | Whether a genuine disagreement remains, and which condition might reconcile it |
| A theory seems stronger than the evidence | Try a minimal example, limiting regime or counterexample family | Whether the claim needs a missing condition, or where the proof route becomes difficult |
| A method changes behavior across settings | Vary a small set of meaningful conditions | Whether the change reflects a mechanism or boundary rather than an arbitrary benchmark |
| Another field has a promising method | Translate its objects, operations and assumptions into this problem | What survives the translation, what fails, and what adaptation would add |
| A field is saturated with small improvements | Ask which behavior, tradeoff, failure or equivalence is still unexplained | A question that produces understanding instead of another small tuning gain |
| A metric seems misleading | Define what users/scientists actually want to measure | Whether the metric tracks it and whether a better measurement changes the conclusions |
| Several explanations seem unrelated | Seek a common model, limiting case or equivalent formulation | What the unification predicts or clarifies beyond renaming known facts |

## Use the moves carefully

For an assumption challenge, distinguish a technical convenience from an essential condition. A theorem restricted to one regime remains valid there; behavior outside that regime motivates a new question.

For an apparent contradiction, inspect the exact constructs and conditions before proposing a new theory. If alignment resolves it, a useful synthesis may remain, but do not advertise an unexplained conflict.

For a counterexample-led question, state the claim and admissible instances first. Propose a construction or bounded search; do not silently execute a large search or treat "no counterexample found" as proof.

For a regime comparison, name the mechanism-bearing axes and scientific outcome. Do not call a smooth crossover a phase transition without evidence, and do not propose an expensive grid before a small diagnostic.

For cross-field borrowing, map the source assumptions and the target operations. A shared word is not a structural match. Compare a proposed adaptation with direct use of the source method and relevant native alternatives. Changing the application alone neither proves nor automatically rules out a contribution.

For a measurement question, require an independent reason the new metric captures the intended construct. A metric that favors the proposed method is not enough.

For a unifying account, say what distinctions disappear, what remain essential, and what new implication or simplification becomes available. A new taxonomy can organize a field; that alone does not establish a new mechanism.

## Reopen when stuck

If all ideas are small variants, change the question or assumption rather than inventing new names. If all are too broad, choose a concrete instance without replacing the user's original problem. If an important question is infeasible now, retain the long-range question and identify an informative accessible subquestion.

After relevant literature or criticism, generate again from the new understanding. Preserve the valuable part of a failed candidate, but do not stack components onto a mechanism whose central premise has failed. The workflow reference explains how to do this within a bounded deeper assignment.
