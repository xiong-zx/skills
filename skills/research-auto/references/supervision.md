# Supervising a research assignment

## Establish the mandate

Identify the final research question and why the user cares, the model or setting being studied, the intended comparison, the current central conjecture or result, confirmed decisions, exclusions, allowed edits, resources, and stopping condition. Keep a compact goal anchor in the existing approved plan or current-state record: the user goal, desired kind of result, model, permitted information, budget and metric. A mechanism explanation, positive algorithm guarantee and lower bound are different deliverables; preserve the one the user selected. Infer routine details from context; ask only about a choice that materially changes direction or scope.

Distinguish the overall project manager, the supervisor of this assignment, and the workers. When they are different agents or chats, the research supervisor owns task selection, bounded execution, reconciliation, and reporting within the assigned direction. It does not silently take over the entire project. In a standalone assignment, the same agent can fill both management roles.

## Let the user choose research directions

For a new open-ended assignment or material replanning, first give a short map of substantively different worthwhile avenues and recommend priorities. Show the distinctions needed for the user's choice; keep detailed prior-work checks, obstacles and route branches in the existing plan until needed. Let the user choose the direction and order before direction-dependent dispatch. Do not narrow a broad goal to one convenient question, manufacture alternatives, or infer novelty from a failed search.

Separate direction selection from execution scope: a bounded worker question makes execution manageable; it does not define the whole research goal or require the user to choose only one direction. Several selected directions may remain active when they are valuable and resources permit. A menu does not authorize launching all its entries or creating parallel workers.

Honor an explicit focused assignment or a previously selected agenda without reopening the choice. Within an approved continuous assignment, ordinary tactics, supporting lemmas and routine corrections remain autonomous; return to the user when the research direction or another material boundary changes. If only one worthwhile avenue survives, explain why instead of manufacturing alternatives. Preparing a proposal is not authorization to start its research.

## Maintain a rolling research program

For substantial open-ended or unattended research, plan at the scale of the assignment. Distinguish the overall research goal, the intended advance during this run, scientific modules that address its main uncertainties, and bounded worker questions. This is a planning distinction, not a mandatory user-facing template. A first theorem, example or source comparison can complete a module without completing the run. An explicitly focused question or review retains its requested scope.

Give the run one or more clear scientific directions and several meaningful modules, with a route from opening exploration through evidence evaluation, subsequent development and final integration. Modules should close distinct uncertainties or complete necessary dependencies; do not manufacture breadth by dividing one derivation into many small lemmas. Permit agents to explore different assumptions, mechanisms or approaches when that can change the conclusion. Choose their number and concurrency for the dependencies and resources, rather than fixing a universal task quota or dispatching everything at once.

Give an open-ended worker a question hard enough to sustain meaningful research, the evidence that would settle it, and the boundaries that protect the user’s goal. Offer starting routes as options and let the worker choose assumptions, constructions and intermediate goals within scope. Keep later stages coarse until evidence makes them actionable. Expected outcomes are hypotheses, not promises. Process material returns when ready instead of waiting for unrelated workers merely to complete a nominal round; then revise priorities, merge complementary routes and retire invalidated ones.

Within a broad modeling or methodological assignment, exploring a candidate assumption is different from adopting it as the final problem. An agent may study an idealized model or neighboring mechanism to understand the original question, provided the changed assumptions and intended relevance are explicit. Do not mistake such exploration for a requirement that the user reselect an already approved broad direction. Equally, a success in the idealized model must not silently replace the original target.

If the user requests review of a new plan before execution, submit it and wait for approval before starting plan-dependent work. This does not cancel independent work already authorized under an existing plan. Within an approved continuous assignment, advance and make evidence-grounded adjustments without requesting approval for each supporting lemma or ending after the first cycle. Changes to the goal, core metric, permitted information, or material model remain user decisions. Ordinary analytic examples, parameter choices and proof tactics within the approved mandate are supervisory choices; do not turn uncertainty between such options into a new approval requirement. For a genuine user decision, state the specific boundary that would change.

## Choose work for its value

Before choosing or extending a technical question, identify:

- which named main obligation or project-level uncertainty it advances;
- what conclusion about the user's goal would change if it succeeded, failed or remained inconclusive;
- why it closes a main dependency or changes a meaningful mechanism or algorithm judgment, rather than merely solving a convenient side problem;
- the cheapest decisive check and the point at which to return to the main analysis.

After the user has selected the direction or directions, choose coherent bounded work packages within that agenda. Each package can include a necessary chain of lemmas, mechanism development, error checks, and algorithmic implications; its scope must not silently narrow the selected agenda. Do not fragment a package into a new dispatch for every formula. Do not equate a smaller constant, a more general spectrum, another assumption, or a completed lemma with research progress unless it changes a meaningful judgment.

Use the user's actual standards concretely; understanding or reproducing a mechanism need not establish novelty or a large practical effect:

- **Novelty:** what is learned beyond the closest verified work or simple baseline? A failed search is not proof of novelty.
- **Argument:** does the problem, obstacle, central idea, and evidence form a coherent explanation? Do not invent a story around unrelated results.
- **Usefulness:** what method, decision, capability, or understanding does the result improve, and under which conditions?

Use the user's actual standard when combining established methods. A composition that closes an assigned modeling, analysis or implementation gap is productive work even when its ingredients are known. Do not make complete novelty or superiority over every plausible baseline an entry requirement unless the assignment calls for it; use prior work to identify reusable results and the real remaining difficulty.

A refutation or negative experiment can be valuable when it rules out a central explanation or changes the next decision. Do not chase a positive theorem after its hypotheses have excluded the difficulty the user wanted to study.

## Keep valuable intermediate results

A useful corollary, mechanism explanation, or intermediate result may warrant a bounded diversion when it closes a main dependency, changes a research or algorithm judgment, or supplies meaningful support within the approved goal. State the payoff, necessary check, and return condition. Integrate a checked result and resume the main work package instead of extending the diversion indefinitely.

For a sustained assignment, treat an initial success as a checkpoint. When the main result is secure, pursue a stronger nearby result if it materially improves the answer, such as a weaker usable condition, a matching obstruction, or a better resource tradeoff. Apply the same fit check to the extra result and reserve time for independent review and integration.

Distinguish supporting results, mechanisms already known, and possible core contributions. Keep their assumptions and verification status visible; do not inflate a supporting result into novelty or a completed project. No cycle must produce a corollary. An early substantive discovery can be reported while the worker continues its remaining main obligations.

## Explain the mechanism before expanding the proof

Start with the smallest model that retains the scientific question. Identify the essential prediction and derive it from first principles before spending on general assumptions, technical lemmas, or uniform guarantees. A toy model or explicit idealization is appropriate when it isolates the mechanism; keep its limits visible.

Use mathematical language with a small set of meaningful quantities. The main explanation should let a reader follow the model, mechanism, and conclusion without navigating a notation dictionary or nested surrogate definitions. Put estimator safeguards, technical constants, uniformity machinery, and full proofs in supporting notes or an appendix; keep load-bearing assumptions, quantifiers, and dependency boundaries in the main text.

Match rigor to the current decision. Exploratory scaling arguments, simple calculations, and heuristic approximations can precede a full theorem when clearly labelled. Check the few implications that distinguish explanations; do not require a complete formal analysis before judging whether the mechanism is worth studying. Essential moment, domain, and information failures must still be exposed, not hidden by simplification.

Do not replace the research goal with whatever is easiest to prove. Generic stability, descent, or convergence bounds may support a structural explanation without establishing it. If discarding a relevant direction, sign, dependence, or history removes the phenomenon, identify the result as support and name the missing mechanism. A smaller certified budget or a longer proof does not close that gap. Recommend changing the immediate proof method or retaining a weaker meaningful result as support; changing the user's goal remains the user's decision.

## Symbolic sufficiency and useful computation

For qualitative or asymptotic theory, default to mechanism and leading terms, symbolic reasoning, then logical audit. Use constants with explicit dependencies, justified O/o notation, and sufficiently large N or sufficiently small step size. An existence claim such as “there is N0 such that for every N >= N0” needs a finite-threshold argument: state which parameters are fixed, what N0 depends on, and whether it is pointwise or uniform over a model class or schedule. Check simultaneous feasibility and the order of parameter choices; do not assume uniformity from pointwise convergence, choose parameters circularly, or discard errors without a valid comparison.

Do not automatically expand every constant, search for a numerical N, or turn this into an all-constants finite-budget certificate. Sampling budgets, fair comparisons, and integer oracle counts must still be mathematically explicit; symbolic N and floor/ceiling expressions often suffice without constructing a numerical instance.

Use numerical or symbolic computation for a bounded question that can change the judgment: a quick error, dimensional, boundary, or special-case check; a counterexample; a mechanism diagnostic; or finite budgets, hyperparameters, and thresholds the user actually needs. Computation supports reasoning, and successful execution does not certify a theorem's logic. Formalization can also prove an existential threshold without computing a numeral; do not equate a Lean proof with explicit numerical certification or launch a separate formalization project without authorization.

## Goal checkpoints and course correction

After every substantive exploration round, material worker return and independent review, compare the actual progress with the original goal before extending or dispatching work. Check again when assumptions, dimensions, observations, controls, comparator, metric or result type change. A correctness audit and this scientific-direction assessment have separate purposes.

Assess privately: which original obligation did the return advance; what did it actually establish; what assumptions or scope changed; and what should the next stage do? A constructed instance establishes occurrence, while an algorithmic guarantee covers its stated class. A proof for a known family does not automatically answer an unknown-model question.

Judge a deviation rather than rejecting it automatically. A necessary supporting result, a mechanism that explains failure, or an alternative that could remove a main obstacle can justify a bounded diversion. State the expected benefit, decisive check and return condition. An attractive calculation with no such connection does not justify another cycle. Distinguish an exploratory change of route from a change to the user's goal, metric, permitted information or resources.

Choose whether to continue, refine, combine, redirect or defer the route, and identify its next contribution to the remaining program. Record material changes and their reason in the existing plan or current-state record; do not create a report or scorecard for every round. Closing one worker task triggers this reassessment, not automatic hand-back or unreviewed redispatch.

Trigger course correction when any of the following is true:

- The next detail cannot be connected to a named main obligation or a decision the result can change.
- A supporting example, counterexample, estimator, exact constant, or technical repair is becoming the main deliverable without that being the user's objective.
- New restrictions make the problem easier by removing its intended difficulty, or silently strengthen a moment/information assumption or replace the metric or comparator.
- A threshold becomes anomalously large, a needed margin vanishingly small, or technical conditions keep accumulating without clarifying the mechanism.
- Two consecutive cycles polish the same auxiliary question without closing a main dependency or changing the scientific judgment. Before another such cycle, supply materially new input and a direct dependency; otherwise redirect. This is not a limit on necessary lemma chains whose cycles make concrete progress toward the main claim.

For inflated thresholds or accumulating conditions, diagnose mechanism and scale first: how large is the effect relative to the agreed baseline, which worst-case bound amplifies the threshold, is the proof pursuing unnecessary precision, and have the assumptions made the effect unimportant for the user's goal? Check whether a simpler model still retains the phenomenon. Consider a different proof method or a weaker meaningful supporting conclusion before polishing constants, scanning larger N, or spawning more candidate tasks. Do not impose a universal requirement for a macroscopic effect or novelty when the user's question is different.

When triggered:

1. Stop extending the detour at a safe boundary; have an active worker save its actual partial result rather than recursively subdivide the side problem.
2. Preserve valid findings as scoped supporting material. Mark an unresolved detail deferred when appropriate; do not call it disproved or discard useful evidence.
3. Return to the earliest unresolved main obligation. Reuse a known result, a conditional lemma, a leading-order estimate or a coarser bound when it suffices for the present decision. Do not spend on sharper constants or a more special construction without a concrete payoff.
4. Give the replacement cycle a decisive test and an explicit stopping/return condition. Once an example has served its diagnostic purpose, return to the general model rather than automatically building a larger example program.
5. Report a material direction correction concisely. Routine corrections inside the approved objective do not require permission. A genuinely different objective, metric, permitted information or material relaxation remains the user's decision; continue independent authorized work while that choice is pending.

Examples and negative results remain appropriate when they are the assigned objective or decide a live main question. For a positive algorithm-analysis assignment, their role is usually diagnostic or explanatory until they feed back into general dynamics, progress, stability or performance analysis. Do not turn one project's correction into a universal ban on constructions or lower bounds.

Keep the goal check and current action in the project's existing state record. Do not create a second dashboard, scoring system or periodic log merely to enforce this procedure.

## Reconcile evidence

Require explicit statements, assumptions, quantifiers, parameter ranges, probability conventions, oracle or information access, cost units, and the actual proof or recorded experiment supporting a candidate. Pin the candidate revision or relevant block hash before independent review; do not create a commit just to obtain a revision identifier.

The supervisor selects and reconciles work; it may do lightweight triage and source checks. Avoid duplicating a worker's full derivation, and do not replace an independent verifier with the supervisor's agreement. A fresh reviewer reconstructs the critical implication from the statement, raw argument, and needed sources without the author's desired verdict. Use the logical-audit priorities in [delegation and models](delegation-and-models.md); numerical certificates are not the default audit product.

Keep proved, restricted, conditional, refuted, empirically observed, and unverified results distinct. Check the requested conclusion, not just successful execution or agreement between agents. Reuse source checks and audits when their inputs and scope are unchanged; review changed dependencies when a candidate is repaired. Read the relevant current sections and decisive raw passages rather than automatically reloading whole historical ledgers. Record material deltas once in the existing state/evidence records; unchanged status checks and repeated hashes are not progress.

While the author is still assessing a foundational assumption, label each new result with the assumption version it uses. Record candidate changes that arise in discussion in the current-state record as candidates, not decisions, and remove superseded terminology from it.

## Reassess and report

Apply the goal checkpoint internally at every substantive cycle boundary before allocating more work. A round's assessment is not automatically a separate report. At the required progress cadence, report meaningful changes concisely; the full route history and evidence stay in the existing records.

Use [Explain Project's common reporting method](../../explain-project/references/report.md): restore the current question and stage when needed, then explain the central change, its key evidence or limitation, and any genuine user choice. Do not turn the research checklist into a long report or require the user to reconstruct progress from worker logs. Repeated questions should improve the next explanation and workflow, not trigger a longer dump of the same material.

Focused requests end after their bounded question and proportionate verification, normally allowing one repair and recheck. Substantial open-ended assignments continue through valuable authorized modules; completing one module is a checkpoint. User-facing brevity creates neither an early stop nor a new approval requirement for routine work. Explicitly continuous work follows the agreed stopping condition and [unattended rules](unattended-research.md).
