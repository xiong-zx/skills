# Guidance used and its limits

Read on 2026-10-27. These are primary university communication/teaching resources, selectively paraphrased into the methods in this skill. They offer practical guidance, not a controlled validation of this workflow or the author's timing.

| Source and relevant part | Method used here | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Stanford Oral Communication Program, Paper ≠ Presentation](https://oralcommprogram.stanford.edu/presentation-and-delivery/paper-presentation), "Moving from Written to Spoken" | Recompose for listeners, use concrete language, simpler syntax and informative transitions | Do not require rhetorical questions, a story spine or repeated summaries on every page |
| [Stanford Oral Communication Program, Delivery](https://oralcommprogram.stanford.edu/presentation-and-delivery/delivery), "Voice" | Distinguish word rate from idea pace; replace repetitive fillers with purposeful pauses | No blanket rule about presentation posture or requiring cue cards instead of the user's requested full script |
| [MIT NSE Communication Lab, Delivery and Q&A](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/nse/commkit/delivery-and-qa/), "Practice", "Speak clearly", "Transition smoothly" | Guide attention verbally, connect adjacent slides and use actual run-throughs to check transitions/time | A message/evidence/reasoning sequence is a useful option, not a mandatory sentence template |
| [MIT EECS Communication Lab, Slide Presentation](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/slideshow/), "Identify Your Purpose", "Connect your work", "Introduce your data" | Adapt to purpose/audience, explain why the next point belongs and orient figure reading | Script work does not authorize its suggested slide edits, force claim titles or impose a single research storyline |
| [MIT OCW 18.821, Presentations](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-821-project-laboratory-in-mathematics-spring-2013/pages/presentations/), "Criteria for Good Presentations", "Chalk Talks versus Slide Presentations" | Use intuition/examples when helpful, retain mathematical accuracy and give listeners time to absorb notation | Undergraduate course/seminar guidance; no adoption of its particular duration or universal omission of proof |
| [Northern Illinois University, Rehearsing the Presentation](https://www.niu.edu/presentations/rehearse/index.shtml) | Allocate time to important parts, rehearse aloud for flow and use observations to revise | A text review is not a rehearsal; recording and audience feedback remain optional user activities |
| [University of Kansas, Public Speaking as Performance, Speaking Occasion](https://opentext.ku.edu/publicspeakingperformance/chapter/speaking-occasion/), "Occasion Element #4", "Delivery Time" | Start from the actual slot, distinguish provisional manuscript estimates from timed delivery | Its general speaking-rate example is not a prescribed rate for mathematical, multilingual or this author's talks |

The natural spoken register, scientific fidelity, one active script and explicit notes-edit authorization are author requirements. The meaning-preservation baseline is [writing-research](../../writing-research/SKILL.md); this skill owns oral language. Deck mechanics remain with [doc-slides](../../doc-slides/SKILL.md). The before/after examples are independently constructed, not quotations from these resources or the author's project.
